In 1973, Bob Dylan contributed music to the film Pat Garrett & Billy the Kid, directed by Sam Peckinpah. The film placed Dylan inside a western world of outlaws, lawmen, aging friendship, and moral exhaustion. The song associated with the project became one of his most recognizable works from that period, but this artwork does not quote its lyrics. Instead, it uses the larger atmosphere: a person standing at the edge of death, a voice becoming simple and direct, and a door that suggests farewell, passage, and the unknown.

The early 1970s were also a moment of transition in Dylan's public identity. He had already moved through folk protest, electric controversy, retreat, country influence, and mythic American storytelling. In this project, Dylan's 1973 moment becomes a cultural doorway: between protest and reflection, between youth movements and older legends, between personal mortality and public legacy.
